Probability Distributions

Learn the main probability distributions, their formulas and how to solve questions involving them.

1. What is a Probability Distribution?

A probability distribution describes how probabilities are assigned to the possible values of a random variable.

There are two main types:

  • Discrete distributions: Used when a random variable takes countable values.
  • Continuous distributions: Used when a random variable can take any value within an interval.

2. Binomial Distribution

The binomial distribution is used when an experiment has a fixed number of independent trials, each with only two possible outcomes: success or failure.

Conditions

  • There is a fixed number of trials (n).
  • Each trial has two possible outcomes.
  • The probability of success (p) remains constant.
  • Trials are independent.

Probability Formula

P(X = x) = C(n,x) p^x (1-p)^(n-x)

Where:

  • n = number of trials
  • x = number of successes
  • p = probability of success
  • 1-p = probability of failure

Mean and Variance

Mean = np

Variance = np(1-p)

Worked Example

A coin is tossed 5 times. Find the probability of getting exactly 3 heads.

Step 1: Identify the values.

n = 5, x = 3, p = 0.5

Step 2: Apply the formula.

P(X = 3) = C(5,3)(0.5)^3(0.5)^2

= 10 × 0.125 × 0.25

= 0.3125

Answer: The probability is 0.3125 or 31.25%.

3. Poisson Distribution

The Poisson distribution is used to calculate the probability of a certain number of events occurring within a fixed interval of time, space or another specified measurement.

Examples include:

  • Number of customers arriving per hour.
  • Number of accidents per month.
  • Number of typing errors per page.

Probability Formula

P(X = x) = (e^-λ × λ^x) / x!

Where:

  • λ (lambda) = average number of events
  • x = number of events required
  • e ≈ 2.71828

Mean and Variance

Mean = λ

Variance = λ

Worked Example

A shop receives an average of 3 customers per hour. Find the probability that exactly 2 customers arrive in an hour.

Step 1: Identify the values.

λ = 3, x = 2

Step 2: Apply the formula.

P(X = 2) = (e^-3 × 3²) / 2!

= (0.0498 × 9) / 2

= 0.2241

Answer: The probability is approximately 0.2241 or 22.41%.

4. Normal Distribution

The normal distribution is a continuous probability distribution that is symmetrical and bell-shaped.

Its two main parameters are:

  • μ (mu) = population mean
  • σ (sigma) = population standard deviation

Standard Normal Formula

Z = (X - μ) / σ

The Z-score tells us how many standard deviations a value is above or below the mean.

Important Properties

  • The curve is symmetrical around the mean.
  • Mean = Median = Mode.
  • The total area under the curve equals 1.
  • Approximately 68% of observations lie within 1 standard deviation of the mean.
  • Approximately 95% lie within 2 standard deviations.
  • Approximately 99.7% lie within 3 standard deviations.

Worked Example

Examination scores are normally distributed with a mean of 60 and a standard deviation of 10. Find the Z-score for a student who scored 80.

Step 1: Identify the values.

X = 80, μ = 60, σ = 10

Step 2: Apply the formula.

Z = (80 - 60) / 10

Z = 20 / 10

Z = 2

Answer: The student's score is 2 standard deviations above the mean.

5. Quick Comparison

  • Binomial: Fixed number of independent trials with two outcomes.
  • Poisson: Counts events occurring within a fixed interval.
  • Normal: Continuous, symmetrical, bell-shaped distribution.

6. Practice Questions

  1. A coin is tossed 6 times. Find the probability of getting exactly 4 heads.
  2. A call centre receives an average of 5 calls per hour. Find the probability of receiving exactly 3 calls in an hour.
  3. Exam scores have a mean of 70 and a standard deviation of 8. Find the Z-score for a student who scores 86.

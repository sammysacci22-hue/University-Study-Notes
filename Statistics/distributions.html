Probability Distributions

Understand how probability distributions work, when to use them, and how to solve questions using their formulas.

01. Introduction to Probability Distributions

A probability distribution describes how probabilities are assigned to the possible values of a random variable.

Types of distributions

Discrete distributions: Used when a random variable takes countable values, such as 0, 1, 2, 3 and so on.

Continuous distributions: Used when a random variable can take any value within an interval, such as height, weight or examination scores.

Remember: Binomial and Poisson are discrete distributions, while the Normal distribution is continuous.

02. Binomial Distribution

The Binomial distribution is used when an experiment consists of a fixed number of independent trials, with each trial having only two possible outcomes: success or failure.

Conditions for using the Binomial distribution

  1. There must be a fixed number of trials (n).
  2. Each trial must have two possible outcomes.
  3. The probability of success (p) must remain constant.
  4. The trials must be independent.

Probability formula

P(X = x) = C(n,x) × px × (1-p)n-x

Where:

  • n = number of trials
  • x = number of successes
  • p = probability of success
  • 1-p = probability of failure
  • C(n,x) = n! / [x!(n-x)!]

Mean and variance

Mean = np

Variance = np(1-p)

Standard deviation = √[np(1-p)]

Worked Example

A fair coin is tossed 5 times. Find the probability of obtaining exactly 3 heads.

Step 1: Identify the values

n = 5

x = 3

p = 0.5

Step 2: Write the formula
P(X = x) = C(n,x)px(1-p)n-x
Step 3: Substitute the values
P(X = 3) = C(5,3)(0.5)3(0.5)2
Step 4: Calculate
= 10 × 0.125 × 0.25

= 0.3125
Final Answer: 0.3125 or 31.25%.

03. Poisson Distribution

The Poisson distribution is used to calculate the probability of a certain number of events occurring within a fixed interval of time, space or another specified measurement.

Examples

  • Number of customers arriving per hour.
  • Number of accidents occurring per month.
  • Number of typing errors per page.

Probability formula

P(X = x) = (e-λ × λx) / x!

Where:

  • λ = average number of events.
  • x = number of events required.
  • e = approximately 2.71828.

Mean and variance

Mean = λ

Variance = λ

Standard deviation = √λ

Worked Example

A shop receives an average of 3 customers per hour. Find the probability that exactly 2 customers arrive in one hour.

Step 1: Identify the values

λ = 3

x = 2

Step 2: Apply the formula
P(X = x) = (e-λ × λx) / x!
Step 3: Substitute
P(X = 2) = (e-3 × 32) / 2!
Step 4: Calculate
= (0.049787 × 9) / 2

= 0.22404
Final Answer: Approximately 0.2240 or 22.40%.

04. Normal Distribution

The Normal distribution is a continuous probability distribution that has a symmetrical, bell-shaped curve.

It is described by two important parameters:

  • μ (mu) = population mean.
  • σ (sigma) = population standard deviation.

Standard normal formula

Z = (X - μ) / σ

The Z-score tells us how many standard deviations a value is above or below the mean.

Important properties

  • The curve is symmetrical around the mean.
  • Mean = Median = Mode.
  • The total area under the curve equals 1.
  • Approximately 68% of observations lie within 1 standard deviation.
  • Approximately 95% lie within 2 standard deviations.
  • Approximately 99.7% lie within 3 standard deviations.

Worked Example

Examination scores are normally distributed with a mean of 60 and a standard deviation of 10. Find the Z-score for a student who scored 80.

Step 1: Identify the values

X = 80

μ = 60

σ = 10

Step 2: Apply the formula
Z = (X - μ) / σ
Step 3: Substitute and calculate
Z = (80 - 60) / 10

Z = 20 / 10

Z = 2
Final Answer: The student's score is 2 standard deviations above the mean.

05. Quick Comparison

Distribution Type Used for
Binomial Discrete Fixed trials with two outcomes.
Poisson Discrete Counting events in an interval.
Normal Continuous Continuous measurements and bell-shaped data.
Exam Tip: Before choosing a formula, identify what the question is describing. Look for fixed trials, event counts or continuous measurements.

06. Practice Questions

Question 1: Binomial

A coin is tossed 6 times. Find the probability of obtaining exactly 4 heads.

n = 6, x = 4, p = 0.5

P(X = 4) = C(6,4)(0.5)4(0.5)2

= 15 × 0.0625 × 0.25

= 0.234375

Question 2: Poisson

A call centre receives an average of 5 calls per hour. Find the probability of receiving exactly 3 calls in an hour.

λ = 5, x = 3

P(X = 3) = (e-5 × 53) / 3!

= 0.14037 approximately

Question 3: Normal

Examination scores have a mean of 70 and a standard deviation of 8. Find the Z-score for a student who scores 86.

Z = (X - μ) / σ

Z = (86 - 70) / 8

Z = 16 / 8

= 2
